# Possible retching welfare checks

## Scope and starting point

Pilot one fixed camera covering one clearly visible public area. When a guest shows a sustained pattern of movement consistent with retching, send a location and short replay to staff. The responder decides whether the guest needs help. A pose sequence alone cannot establish vomiting, intoxication, or its cause.

The staff-app baseline is `335b680`. Its SQLite store already persists a signal and unique episode ID, while the history, web copy, resolution choices and rehearsal clips assume a person-down check. The pose runner and capture evaluator exist separately on `main` at `087aef6`. Before wiring live capture, reconcile with the team's integrated runner and event contract. Reuse its camera reader and pose inference pass. The staff workflow and replay evaluation can proceed independently.

## Build order

1. **Prove the staff workflow with a fixture.** Add a labeled rehearsal event with signal `possible_retching`, stable episode ID, camera, zone, landmark, source and detection time. Send it through `IncidentStore.create_incident`. Introduce a signal-to-presentation mapping for notifications, queue, pins/detail, history and recently resolved checks. Show “Guest may need assistance” and “Possible retching motion.” Source allowed outcomes from the server per signal. Use “Assistance provided,” “False alarm” and “Couldn't verify” for this check. Preserve existing person-down rows, outcomes and claim rules. Select demonstration clips by signal and zone so a retching check never shows a fall clip.
2. **Build a replay baseline.** With consenting teammates, record a few seconds before and after acted retching in the intended lighting. Include coughing, laughing, dancing, drinking, tying shoes, picking up objects and staff clearing tables. Label onset, end, zone and whether the torso stayed visible. Reserve entire people and recording sessions for evaluation. Keep raw footage under ignored local storage. Use staged movement only.
3. **Score a sequence on the same person.** Build a separate timestamp-driven detector alongside the fall detector. Start with a transparent baseline using shoulder-to-hip angle, head movement relative to shoulders, and repeated forward contractions over a short window. Compare against a simple bend-only baseline on the same clips. Tune confidence, repetition and window duration on development data. The current primary-person selector chooses the highest-confidence detection each frame. Only accumulate evidence with a continuous matching subject in the configured area. Ambiguous crossings, missing keypoints or stale frames invalidate pending evidence. For this first pilot, abstain when multiple people cannot be distinguished. If the baseline confuses everyday motions, evaluate a short-window action classifier offline before any wider rollout.
4. **Connect confirmed episodes and replay.** Emit one event per episode, with the same ID on delivery retries. Keep the episode latched through occlusion and staff resolution. Rearm after sustained visible recovery on the same track. Use capture timestamps for elapsed time and UTC for the stored incident. Feed live and recorded observations through the same detector. Retain a bounded pre-event buffer and attach a short post-event segment to the incident ID. Serve real clips through an authenticated staff route from local storage with an agreed deletion period. A missing clip or camera disconnect must still allow a responder to finish the check. Release behind an off-by-default setting until the rehearsal gate passes.
5. **Verify on the actual phone and laptop.** Test that repeated movement yields one alert, a single bend yields none, gaps cannot join two people into one episode, and recovery allows a second alert. Retry the same incident against SQLite. Verify one winner on simultaneous claims, assignment after refresh, signal-appropriate resolution, correct replay after polling, and unchanged fall behavior. Confirm the phone shows the zone pin and the laptop shows the same check and owner.

## Decision before live alerts

Suggested hackathon rehearsal gate, subject to the venue team's alert tolerance: detect at least 8 of 10 held-out staged episodes, produce zero duplicate alerts, and at most one false alert in two camera-hours of ordinary staged activity. Report counts with denominators, including missed or obscured episodes, false alerts per camera-hour, time from labeled onset to phone alert, and effect on fall-detector throughput. Record camera placement, lighting, actor count and device. This small rehearsal qualifies a demo only. If the gate is missed, keep the feature in labeled replay or silent evaluation and investigate the most common failure before tuning again.
