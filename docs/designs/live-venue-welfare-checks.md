## Problem and demo

A camera can surface a possible welfare incident, but venue staff still need to know where to go and who has taken responsibility. Inspired by Shire Intelligence’s floor view, the demo turns a visible event into a welfare check on a live venue map. A judge claims it on a phone and watches the status update for everyone.

## Design decisions

- **One live source.** A fixed phone films consenting actors in a controlled area framed away from the audience. A laptop processes the feed and creates one check when a person goes down and stays on the floor. A consented rehearsal clip can run through the same detector as a clearly labeled fallback. The camera describes what it saw. Staff determine what help is needed.
- **A venue map for the supervisor.** Configure the camera’s area and landmark ahead of time, for example East bar beside the side stairs. The live incident pin marks that area. Selecting a pin opens the same check shown in the queue. The checked location is also visible on the responder’s phone.
- **A shared queue for responders.** Staff see actionable checks, with unclaimed ones first. Anyone on the responder team can claim an unowned check. The server grants one owner even if two people tap Accept together. Every screen shows the owner and follows the same steps: **Needs responder → Accepted → Arrived → Resolved**. The server permits arrival and resolution only for the owner. An unclaimed check becomes overdue after the configured interval from creation. Accepting it clears that flag.
- **One check drives both views.** Repeated observations of the same down episode belong to the same incident, even if staff resolve it while the actor is still down. The detector can open another check after it sees the actor upright for a sustained interval and then observes a distinct down episode. Losing sight of the actor does not reset the detector. The queue and map use the same incident record and history. On first open, refresh, or reconnect, both views load current server state. The phone shows the location and the next action. The supervisor sees all open checks and their latest status.
- **Hackathon scope.** One newly detected incident, one mapped camera, and a browser-based responder and supervisor view. A labeled sample check can populate another zone. Multiple live feeds, automatic dispatch, and drink-tampering detection are later work.

## What success looks like

- Show ordinary bending or sitting without creating a check. Stage a person safely lowering themselves to the floor and remaining down. Expect one new check at the east bar on all open screens.
- Have two responder sessions attempt to claim it. Show one owner everywhere. Refresh the owner’s phone and verify it still shows the check and allows the next action. Mark arrival, then resolve it and confirm it leaves the active queue and map.
- Leave a rehearsal check unclaimed long enough to verify the supervisor flag. Measure time from detection to appearance on the responder screen and show the actual demo result.
- Enter Agent Solution as the working track. Explain the observation, persistence rule, and shared state changes, and show the detector’s measured processing rate during rehearsal.
